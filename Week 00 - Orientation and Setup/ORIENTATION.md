# Orientation — what this module is, and how it runs

Talking points for the first 40 minutes, before installation starts.

---

## Welcome

Welcome to **CST1510: Python Programming and Applications** — a 12-week
module designed to take you from a complete beginner to a confident
programmer capable of building interactive applications and dashboards
with Python. Whether your focus is **AI / Data Science**, **Cyber
Security**, or **IT**, the first seven weeks build the same foundation
for everyone; your chosen field is where the second half of the module
takes you.

### What you will gain

- **Core programming skills** — a solid grounding in Python, from values
  and types through to functions, data structures, and object-oriented
  code, forming the basis for everything technical that follows in your
  degree.
- **Practical problem-solving** — the ability to break a problem down,
  design a systematic solution, and implement it correctly in code —
  practised weekly, in a real lab, not just described in a lecture.
- **Data and application skills** — hands-on experience with structured
  data (`pandas`), real APIs, password hashing and authentication, and
  SQL, with the tools to embed all of it inside a working application
  rather than as isolated exercises.
- **A portfolio project** — from Week 8, you design, build, and grow one
  **Streamlit application** in your chosen lane, ending in a real,
  deployed dashboard with a public URL you can put on a CV.
- **Confidence in coding** — the foundation and the practical mileage to
  take on more advanced programming challenges and specialise further in
  your field.

By the end of CST1510, you will not only understand the Python language —
you will have used it to build something real: a working application,
developed and deployed by you, that demonstrates both technical
competence and professional relevance.

## What CST1510 actually is

Twelve weeks. Not twelve topics bolted together — one system, built one box
at a time, using real tools the whole way. By the end you will have hashed a
real password, broken and fixed a real SQL injection, pulled data from a
real API, and deployed a real, working app with a public URL.

You are on this module whether you're heading into AI/Data Science, Cyber
Security, or IT — three different degrees, one shared foundation. That's
deliberate: an AI/DS graduate who never had to think about SQL injection,
or an IT graduate who never touched a DataFrame, is missing something. The
first seven weeks are the same for everyone. From Week 8, you pick a lane.

## The three lanes

- **AI / Data Science**
- **Cyber Security**
- **IT**

You do **not** choose now. In Weeks 1–7, your weekly mini-project can be in
any lane, or a different one each week — try things. The lane only locks in
from Week 8, when you start the one project you'll carry to the end of
term.

## The shape of the two courseworks

**CW1 — Weeks 1 to 7.** Every week ends in a small, disposable project:
built in the lab, tiered Threshold / Typical / Excellent, and then you move
on — nothing carries over to next week. What *does* carry over is a single
page you build by hand, one box per week, called the **Cheat Sheet** — by
the end of Week 7 it has seven boxes on it, in your own handwriting, and
it's part of what you submit.

**CW2 — Weeks 8 to 12.** One project. You pick your lane in Week 8 and grow
the *same* codebase every week after that — pulling real data, adding a
login, moving into a real database, building a dashboard, and shipping it
with a public URL. An AI-assisted feature is optional; deploying it is not.
There's no journal and no Cheat Sheet this half — commit and push your
progress every week, and what you submit at the end is the project code, a
short technical report, and a video explaining your own understanding of
the code.

Deadlines, exact grading weight, and how you submit are **not** in any
week's material — they're in `RESOURCES/CW1-spec.md` and
`RESOURCES/CW2-spec.md`, which is the one place to check.

## How a normal week runs

- **Workshop (2 hours)** — a running demo first, so you see the finished
  thing before any explanation. Then four short topics, each taught in
  small chunks with a "your turn" straight after every idea.
- **Lab (3 hours)** — starts with three *deliberately broken* files you
  have to fix. That's on purpose: reading an error and knowing what it
  means is a skill this module treats as seriously as writing the code in
  the first place. Then drills, then your tiered mini-project, then a
  two-minute demo to the person next to you and a commit and push before
  you leave. In Weeks 1–7 you also fill in and photograph that week's box
  on the Cheat Sheet — the rest of the journal (the diagram, three
  concepts, your errors) stays on paper for yourself and isn't submitted.
  From Week 8 there's no journal or Cheat Sheet at all — just the project,
  growing every week.

Every week follows this same shape. Once you've done it once, you know how
every week after it works.

## Two things that stay separate all term

| | What it is | Who builds it |
|---|---|---|
| **The demo** | one program, revisited weekly, getting better as your toolkit grows | shown to you in the workshop |
| **Your work** | your own version of that week's idea | you, in the lab |

Watching the demo and building your own version are different activities —
the demo is there so you see where you're headed before you start.

## What this module expects from you

- **Bring a laptop that's actually set up** — that's what today is for.
- **Errors are normal, every single week.** The lab starts with broken code
  on purpose. Nobody is checking whether you get it right first time.
- **The Cheat Sheet is handwritten, on paper, photographed.** Not typed,
  not copied from a slide — the point is building it yourself, one week at
  a time, so by Week 7 it's genuinely yours.
- **Nothing here is a toy example.** The password hashing is real bcrypt.
  The SQL injection actually works, and actually gets fixed. The dashboard
  you ship in Week 11 gets a real URL you can put on a CV. Treat it that
  way.
